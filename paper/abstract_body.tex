A Mixture-of-Experts model too large for one consumer GPU keeps most of its experts in host memory, and decoding one
token at a time then waits on host reads. We ask how fast such decoding could be on a given machine, where the
remaining time goes, and what knowing the future routing is worth. Our bound is the host reads of Belady's MIN with
bypass, the fewest that any cache holding $C$ experts per layer can make, at the machine's probed read rate. Our
llama.cpp expert cache, faster than two other systems at most configurations, reaches
\dcShareMinLowMin--\dcShareMinLowMax\% of the bound's speed on machines with consumer processors at the smallest budget
we test. We call its time beyond the bound the gap. Oracles built into the engine close about half of the gap; we
attribute the rest without measuring it part by part. In our engine, at that budget and on machines whose link reads at
least half as fast as the CPU, caching the experts MIN's greedy schedule keeps pays only if each admitted expert is read
once, not twice. Together the two changes close \dmNewTogetherLow\% of the gap in a test registered before its machines were
rented. There, policies without foresight, built from the engine's own mechanisms, recover at most \olCapLowMax\% of what an
oracle that reads ahead gains. In host reads, half of foresight's value needs the routing of about \dcMed$\,C$ distinct
experts per layer ahead.
