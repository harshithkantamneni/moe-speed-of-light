A Mixture-of-Experts model too large for one consumer GPU keeps most of its experts in host memory, and decoding one
token at a time then waits on host reads. We bound how fast such decoding can be on a given machine, measure where the
remaining time goes, and price knowledge of the future routing. The bound is the fewest host reads any expert cache of
a given size can make (Belady's MIN with bypass) at the machine's measured read rate. Our llama.cpp expert cache,
faster than two other systems at most settings, reaches \dcShareMinLowMin--\dcShareMinLowMax\% of the bound's speed on
consumer machines at the smallest cache we test. Oracles built into the engine close about half of the remaining time.
Caching the experts MIN would keep pays only if the engine keeps them: its in-step fetches, which copy misses into
cache slots the policy did not choose, push them out. With those fetches off, MIN's set runs \hsGreedyZeroLowMin{} to
\hsPlanZeroLowMax{} times as fast as the deployed cache without them on two machines, even when every cached expert is
read twice. Policies
without foresight recover at most \olCapLowMax\% of an oracle's gain. Half of foresight's value needs the routing of
about \dcMed{} cache-fulls of distinct experts ahead. Every prediction was committed before its machines were rented.
