A Mixture-of-Experts model too large for one consumer GPU keeps most of its experts in host memory, and decoding one
token at a time then waits on host reads. We bound how fast such decoding can be on a given machine, measure where the
remaining time goes, and price knowledge of the future routing. The bound is the fewest host reads any expert cache of
a given size can make (Belady's MIN with bypass) at the machine's probed read rate. Our llama.cpp expert cache,
faster than two other systems at most settings, reaches \dcShareMinLowMin--\dcShareMinLowMax\% of the bound's speed on
consumer machines at the smallest cache we test. Oracles built into the engine split the remaining time by what is
cached, how it is read and when. The engine's fetch table, which copies misses into cache slots the policy did not
choose, hides the value of caching MIN's set: with it, the set pays only if each admitted expert is also read once;
without it, on \dzNWord{} machines, the set alone closes \dmCpuSetaloneZeroLow\% of the gap and both changes together
\dmCpuTogetherZeroLow\%. Even the read-ahead oracle leaves about half of the gap. On five fast-link machines, policies
without foresight recover at most \olCapLowMax\% of its gain. In trace replay, half of foresight's value needs the
routing of about \dcMed{} cache-fulls of distinct experts ahead. Registered predictions were committed before their
machines were rented; the consumer range and the replay result are post hoc.
