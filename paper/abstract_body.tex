A Mixture-of-Experts model too large for one consumer GPU keeps most of its experts in host memory, and decoding one
token at a time then waits on host reads. We bound how fast such decoding can be on a given machine, measure where the
remaining time goes, and price knowledge of the future routing. The bound is the host reads of Belady's MIN with bypass,
the fewest that any cache holding $C$ experts per layer can make, at the machine's probed read rate. Our llama.cpp
expert cache, faster than two other systems at most configurations, reaches \dcShareMinLowMin--\dcShareMinLowMax\% of
the bound's speed on machines with consumer processors at the smallest budget we test. We call its time beyond the
bound the gap. Oracles inside the engine close about half of the gap; we attribute the rest, not measure it. At that
budget, on machines whose link reads at least half as fast as the CPU, caching the experts MIN would keep pays only if
each admitted expert is also read once instead of twice; the two changes together close \dmNewTogetherLow\% of the gap
in a test registered before its machines were rented. There, four policies without foresight, built from the engine's
own mechanisms, recover at most \olCapLowMax\% of the gain of an oracle that reads ahead. In host reads,
half of foresight's value needs the routing of about \dcMed$\,C$ distinct experts per layer ahead.
