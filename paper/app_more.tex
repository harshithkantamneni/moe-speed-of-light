\paragraph{Admitting less, in detail.} The margin $\kappa$ was chosen as the value that minimises the deployed path's
reads on each model's mixed-domain text, not the AIME text (\texttt{scripts/online\_admit.py}): 3 at the smallest budget
and 2 at 25\%. It cuts the deployed cache's host reads by \jiDkReadsGLowMin--\jiDkReadsGLowMax\% at gpt-oss 11\% and
\jiDkReadsGMidMin--\jiDkReadsGMidMax\% at 25\%. On the three machines of job 106 that ran stably it ran
\jiDkGLowMin--\jiDkGLowMax$\times$ and \jiDkGMidMin--\jiDkGMidMax$\times$ the deployed cache (Qwen3:
\jiDkQLowMin--\jiDkQMidMax$\times$; \cref{tab:job106all}). We had registered at least 1.01 at gpt-oss on every host: it
fell short on two of the three stable ones, and the registered pooled median of at least 1.02 held only through the
two unstable machines (\jiDkPooled{} on the stable ones). On the \jlValidWord{} valid machines of job 107 it ran
\jlDkMin--\jlDkMax$\times$ (median \jlDkMed), below the registered 0.995 at \jlDkFailWord{} cell, the server at gpt-oss
25\%. Predicted from each configuration's own counters, \cref{eq:sum} gives these ratios within a median
\jiRatioPredMed{} on job 106's stable machines, but predicting no change does almost as well (\jiNoChangeMed), and on
job 107's server it missed the registered 0.03 at \jlDkPredFailN{} cells. A learned reuse predictor with the same kind
of margin (job 097's, fitted on other text) reads less still (\jiLrnReadsGLowMin--\jiLrnReadsGMidMax\% fewer) but costs
\jiLrnUsGMin--\jiLrnUsGMax\,$\mu$s of host time per token on gpt-oss and runs \jiLrnGLowMin--\jiLrnGMidMax$\times$: it pays
where host memory is slow and loses where it is fast, against the registered prediction that it would not lose.

\paragraph{The slowest link.} On the EPYC 7302 host of job 106, whose link moves \jiVsLink\,GB/s, the fewest-admission
set copied in the step lost in each round (\jiVsPlanMin--\jiVsPlanMax$\times$); that host computed wrong outputs
throughout, so we draw nothing from it, but below some link rate even the fewest-admission set should not be copied in
the step.

\paragraph{The machine decides how much.} Across machines, the gain of every state that moves reads from the CPU to
the link follows the link-to-CPU ratio (Spearman over the machines that ran it through job 104, 95\% bootstrap
interval): MIN copied in the step \rcFetchRatio{} [\rcFetchRatioLo, \rcFetchRatioHi] (\rcFetchRatioN{} machines), copied
ahead \rcPacedRatio{} [\rcPacedRatioLo, \rcPacedRatioHi] (\rcPacedRatioN), admitting every miss \rcAaRatio{}
[\rcAaRatioLo, \rcAaRatioHi] (\rcAaRatioN). MIN loaded by the CPU, which reads every admission twice and copies it in
the background, tracks the link's absolute rate more closely (\rcBypassBp{} [\rcBypassBpLo, \rcBypassBpHi],
\rcBypassBpN{} machines, against \rcBypassRatio{} for the ratio; the two intervals overlap, and the feature was picked
from a search over all probe features). The greedy schedule copied in the step gains
\jeNewFetchLowMin--\jeNewFetchLowMax$\times$ at 11\% on \jeNewN{} further machines with ratios \jeNewRatioMin{} and
\jeNewRatioMax{}, and loses only on the machines whose link reads at under a third of their CPU rate; made ahead of use,
the same copies lose beyond their interval on no machine they ran on (at worst \jbPfPaced$\times$, on Pf).

\paragraph{Machines differ more than problems.} Problems rank largely the same on every machine: Kendall's coefficient
of concordance of MIN-copied-in-the-step's per-problem gain over \ppMachines{} machines and \ppProblems{} problems is
\ppWFetchLow{} at 11\% and \ppWFetchMid{} at 25\%, and a problem's gain follows how far the deployed policy's reads
exceed MIN's on its trace (Spearman \ppRhoFetchLow{} at 11\%, \ppRhoFetchMid{} at 25\%). Yet machines carry
\ppVarHostsFetchMax\% of the variance of its log gain and problems \ppVarProbFetchMin--\ppVarProbFetchMax\%.
