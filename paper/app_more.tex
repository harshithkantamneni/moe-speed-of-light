loaded by the CPU (\MinTwo, \FewTwo) or in the step (\MinOne, \FewOne), as the greedy rule picks it or as the schedule with the fewest admissions does. On the

the step as the greedy one, for \jfMissDevMin--\jfMissDevMax\% more misses (more than its replay predicts; its measured
gains include that cost). On every launch it gains over the deployed cache with either load and at both budgets, each
95\% interval above 1; on the machine with the fastest link, \FewTwo{} is \jhFastBypassMidBehind{} behind

\paragraph{The probe's rate, and machines as the unit.} $B_{\mathrm{host}}$ is the highest single reading of one probe
run. Its second-highest reading is a median \rbSecondGapMed\% lower and more than 5\% lower on \rbSecondGapOverFive{} of
\rbLaunches{} launches (\rbLaunchMachines{} machines): launch \rbSecondGapLaunch{} (\cref{tab:job107}), by \rbSecondGapMax\%
(\cref{tab:robust}). With the second-highest reading,
\cref{eq:sum} is within 6\% on \rbWithinSixSecond{} instead of \rbWithinSixMax{} of the \rbConsLow{} consumer launches at
gpt-oss 11\%; with the best CPU-only reading, which ignores that the link reads at the same time, on
\rbWithinSixCpu. By \cref{eq:sum}'s own accounting the engine reads more than 5\% faster than the highest reading on
\rbAboveMax{} of \rbLaunchBudgets{} launch-budgets (up to \rbAboveMaxMax$\times$), so the bounds are relative to the probe.
Taking the machine as the unit, one value per machine: \FewOne{} gains a mean
\rbPlanMean$\times$ (95\% $t$-interval over machines \rbPlanLo--\rbPlanHi) on the \rbPlanMachines{} machines that ran stably;
\Margin, each machine's mean over the two gpt-oss budgets, \rbDkMean$\times$ (\rbDkLo--\rbDkHi) on
\rbDkMachines. \Cref{eq:sum}'s error is a mean \rbErrMean\% (\rbErrLo{} to
\rbErrHi\%) at gpt-oss 11\% over the \rbErrMachines{} consumer machines, and +\rbErrMidMean\% (\rbErrMidLo{} to
\rbErrMidHi\%) at 25\%; on the \rbErrFoundMachines{} machines it was found on, \rbErrFoundMean\% (\rbErrFoundLo{} to
\rbErrFoundHi\%) at 11\%: of the \rbFoundN{} launches it was found on, it under-predicts \rbFoundUnder{} at 11\% and
over-predicts \rbFoundOverMid{} at 25\%. $G$ was profiled on the machine for \rbConsProf{} of the \rbConsAll{} consumer
launches; the others use the median of the profiles. The median $|$error$|$ is \rbProfErrMed\% on the launches with
their own $G$ and \rbMedGErrMed\% on the others, but the profiled launches all come from the registered tests.

\paragraph{Admitting less, in detail.} The margin $\kappa$ of \Margin{} was chosen as the value that minimises the deployed path's
reads on each model's mixed-domain text, not the AIME text (\texttt{scripts/online\_admit.py}): 3 at the smallest budget
and 2 at 25\%. It cuts the deployed cache's host reads by \jiDkReadsGLowMin--\jiDkReadsGLowMax\% at gpt-oss 11\% and
\jiDkReadsGMidMin--\jiDkReadsGMidMax\% at 25\%. On the three machines of job 106 that ran stably it ran
\jiDkGLowMin--\jiDkGLowMax$\times$ and \jiDkGMidMin--\jiDkGMidMax$\times$ the deployed cache (Qwen3:
\jiDkQLowMin--\jiDkQMidMax$\times$; \cref{tab:job106all}). We had registered at least 1.01 at gpt-oss on every machine: it
fell short on two of the three stable ones, and the registered pooled median of at least 1.02 held only through the
two unstable machines (\jiDkPooled{} on the stable ones). On the \jlValidWord{} valid machines of job 107 it ran
\jlDkMin--\jlDkMax$\times$ (median \jlDkMed), below the registered 0.995 at \jlDkFailWord{} cell, the server at gpt-oss
25\%. Predicted from each configuration's own counters, \cref{eq:sum} gives these ratios within a median
\jiRatioPredMed{} on job 106's stable machines, but predicting no change does almost as well (\jiNoChangeMed), and on
job 107's server it missed the registered 0.03 at \jlDkPredFailN{} cells. A learned reuse predictor with the same kind
of margin (job 097's, fitted on other text) reads less still (\jiLrnReadsGLowMin--\jiLrnReadsGMidMax\% fewer) but costs
\jiLrnUsGMin--\jiLrnUsGMax\,$\mu$s of host time per token on gpt-oss and runs \jiLrnGLowMin--\jiLrnGMidMax$\times$: it pays
where host memory is slow and loses where it is fast, against the registered prediction that it would not lose.

\paragraph{The slowest link.} On the EPYC 7302 machine of job 106, whose link moves \jiVsLink\,GB/s,
\FewOne{} lost in each round (\jiVsPlanMin--\jiVsPlanMax$\times$); that machine computed wrong outputs
throughout, so we draw nothing from it, but below some link rate even the fewest-admission set should not be copied in
the step.

\paragraph{The machine decides how much.} Across machines, the gain of every state that moves reads from the CPU to
the link follows the link-to-CPU ratio (Spearman over the machines that ran it through job 104, 95\% bootstrap
interval): \MinOne{} \rcFetchRatio{} [\rcFetchRatioLo, \rcFetchRatioHi] (\rcFetchRatioN{} machines), the read-ahead
oracle \rcPacedRatio{} [\rcPacedRatioLo, \rcPacedRatioHi] (\rcPacedRatioN), admitting every miss \rcAaRatio{}
[\rcAaRatioLo, \rcAaRatioHi] (\rcAaRatioN). \MinTwo, which reads every admission twice and copies it in
the background, tracks the link's absolute rate more closely (\rcBypassBp{} [\rcBypassBpLo, \rcBypassBpHi],
\rcBypassBpN{} machines, against \rcBypassRatio{} for the ratio; the two intervals overlap, and the feature was picked
from a search over all probe features). \MinOne{} gains
\jeNewFetchLowMin--\jeNewFetchLowMax$\times$ at 11\% on \jeNewN{} further machines with ratios \jeNewRatioMin{} and
\jeNewRatioMax{}, and loses only on the machines whose link reads at under a third of their CPU rate; issued ahead of use,
as in the read-ahead oracle, the same copies lose beyond their interval on no machine they ran on (at worst \jbPfPaced$\times$, on Pf).

\paragraph{Machines differ more than problems.} Problems rank largely the same on every machine: Kendall's coefficient
of concordance of \MinOne's per-problem gain over \ppMachines{} machines and \ppProblems{} problems is
\ppWFetchLow{} at 11\% and \ppWFetchMid{} at 25\%, and a problem's gain follows how far the deployed policy's reads
exceed MIN's on its trace (Spearman \ppRhoFetchLow{} at 11\%, \ppRhoFetchMid{} at 25\%). Yet machines carry
\ppVarHostsFetchMax\% of the variance of its log gain and problems \ppVarProbFetchMin--\ppVarProbFetchMax\%.
