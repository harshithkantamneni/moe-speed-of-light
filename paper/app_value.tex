\paragraph{The window policies.} At step $g$ the engine plans the step's misses before it runs, from the trace's record
of step $g$ (the routers give it layer by layer in a deployable system) and from a view of records $g{+}1..g{+}W$ of the
same problem. A miss is processed in request order: it takes a free slot; otherwise the victim is a resident not
requested now that is not seen in the window (lowest decayed score), or, if every resident is seen, the one seen
furthest; a miss seen in the window is admitted unless the victim is seen sooner, and a miss not seen is admitted if
the victim is not seen either (admit every miss beyond the window; with the deployed margin $\kappa{=}1$, only if its
score beats the victim's by one). Admissions are fetched in the step; every miss is read once. In a degraded view each
expert of a future record is kept with probability $r$, decided by a hash of (seed, $g$, record, layer, position), so
step $g$ sees one view throughout and step $g{+}1$ an independent one; a dropped expert is replaced by a uniformly drawn
one (fill: precision and recall $r$) or left out (drop: recall $r$, precision 1). \texttt{scripts/value\_map.py}
replays the plan bit for bit: on a CPU build the engine's reads equal the replay's in 30 configurations at three budgets
(\texttt{cputest099}), and on the panel's GPUs, where the routing in the run can differ from the recorded trace by
numerics, within \pnReplayMaxLow\% at gpt-oss 11\%.

\paragraph{What the table says.} Horizon and accuracy trade: at gpt-oss 11\% an 8-token window at recall 0.5 closes
\vmEightHalfLow{} of the gap, about what two exact tokens close (\vmShareTwoLow), and the whole future at recall 0.5
closes \vmAllHalfLow. Fill and drop cost about the same (the largest difference in any cell is \vmFillDropMax). A larger
cache needs a longer window: at 25\% the same exact 4-token window closes \vmShareFourMid{} of the gap, against
\vmShareFourLow{} at 11\%.

\paragraph{A realisable forecaster.} Ridge regression from the all-layer routing of step $t$ to that of step $t{+}h$
($h{=}1..8$), fitted on each model's mixed-domain own text and applied to the AIME routing, forecasts the top $k$ experts
of each layer with a precision of \lfPrecOneGpt{} one token ahead and \lfPrecFourGpt{} four ahead on gpt-oss
(\lfPrecOneQwen{} and \lfPrecFourQwen{} on Qwen3); a decayed request count, the online policy's own signal, scores
\lfPrecDecOneGpt{} and \lfPrecDecFourGpt. Driving the window policy, the ridge forecasts close
\lfShareMinGpt--\lfShareMaxGpt\% of the gap on gpt-oss and \lfShareMinQwen--\lfShareMaxQwen\% on Qwen3 over windows
of 1--8 tokens (\texttt{prereg/forecaster\_linear.json}): far less than a random-error window of similar precision,
because its correct forecasts are mostly the experts the cache already holds. A sequence model reports recall above
90\% one token ahead with $k{+}3$ candidates~\cite{wang2026seqmoe}; its precision is then at most $k/(k{+}3)$, 0.57 for
gpt-oss and 0.73 for Qwen3, and it has not been reproduced.
