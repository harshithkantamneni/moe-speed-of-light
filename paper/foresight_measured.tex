\paragraph{Foresight, measured.} We then gave the engine foresight of future routing and measured what it is worth
(\cref{fig:foresight}; another rented RTX 5090, a Ryzen 9 9950X whose memory reads \fsmHostCpu\,GB/s by the CPU,
\fsmHostLink{} over the link and \fsmHostBoth{} by both, weaker than the stock-clock host's). An oracle policy reads
the routing of the next $W$ decode steps from a trace recorded on the same machine over the same teacher-forced text,
admits the experts those steps will route to, soonest first, and evicts the resident whose next use is furthest
(Belady within the window); the copies ride the admission path, paced in 16\,MB pieces (pacing the online policy
itself changes it by under 1\%), and an expert still in flight at its step runs on the CPU like any other miss. This
is the hit-optimal oracle, not the bytes-optimal foresight the accounting assumes: it admits an expert for one future
use as readily as for ten. With the whole sequence in view it runs \fsmRatioGMid$\times$ [\fsmRatioLoGMid,
\fsmRatioHiGMid] the online policy at gpt-oss 25\% and \fsmRatioQMid$\times$ [\fsmRatioLoQMid, \fsmRatioHiQMid] at
Qwen3 25\%, closing \fsmClosedGMid{} and \fsmClosedQMid\% of the gap to this host's limit; its speed-up is
\fsmRecoveredGMid{} and \fsmRecoveredQMid\% of what the foresight-only term $v(F)$ predicts, reached while reading
nearly the online policy's experts per token (\fsmReadsOracleGMid{} against \fsmReadsBaseGMid; \fsmReadsOracleQMid{}
against \fsmReadsBaseQMid). At the two GPU-bound cells, where the accounting ranks foresight second on listing B and
level with overlap on this host, it runs \fsmRatioGHigh$\times$ [\fsmRatioLoGHigh, \fsmRatioHiGHigh] and
\fsmRatioQHigh$\times$ [\fsmRatioLoQHigh, \fsmRatioHiQHigh]. Half of the gain at gpt-oss 25\% arrives between
$W{=}4$ and 16 (\fsmWfiftyGMid{} tokens by log interpolation), near the 10 its trace gives in \cref{sec:traces} (the
fit: 9.3); at Qwen3 25\%, \fsmWfiftyQMid{} (trace 3.9, fit 3.7). Where the host binds hardest the same oracle
\emph{loses}: \fsmLossRange\% at gpt-oss 11\% and Qwen3 12.5\% with the whole sequence in view, and at every window
(\fsmNegWinMin--\fsmNegWinMax$\times$). There it reads more host bytes than the online policy,
\fsmReadsOracleGLow{} and \fsmReadsOracleQLow{} experts per token (misses plus admissions) against
\fsmReadsBaseGLow{} and \fsmReadsBaseQLow, because Belady admits an expert used once where the optimum, at
\fsmReadsOptGLow{} and \fsmReadsOptQLow{} reads per token, bypasses it; and its copies compete with the helpers for
the same memory and land late (\fsmHitOracleGLow{} and \fsmHitOracleQLow\% hits against the optimum's
\fsmHitOptGLow{} and \fsmHitOptQLow\%). The accounting's term is the optimum's reads, foresight in bytes, which a
hit-optimal oracle does not supply\fsBytesPointer. Two other parts of the model are probed on the way. Turning off
the engine's within-layer overlap of a layer's CPU misses with its GPU hits changes speed by under 1\% at every cell,
so the overlap the accounting charges must come from across the step, and from rebalancing, not from within a
layer. And the law, with its GPU term calibrated on the online run, evaluated on the oracle runs' own counters
over-predicts their time by \fsmLawErrPosMin--\fsmLawErrPosMax\% at the four cells where the oracle gains: the
admissions' reads overlapped the step, which the law charges in sequence. Foresight brings that overlap with it.
