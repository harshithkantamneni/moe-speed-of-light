\paragraph{Foresight, measured.} We then gave the engine the foresight the accounting assumes and measured what it is
worth (\cref{fig:foresight}; a third RTX 5090 host, a Ryzen 9 9950X whose memory reads \fsmHostCpu\,GB/s by the CPU,
\fsmHostLink{} over the link and \fsmHostBoth{} by both). An oracle policy reads the routing of the next $W$ decode
steps from a trace recorded on the same machine over the same teacher-forced text, admits the experts those steps will
route to, soonest first, and evicts the resident whose next use is furthest (Belady within the window); the copies
ride the normal admission path, and an expert still in flight at its step runs on the CPU as any miss. This is the
hit-optimal oracle: it admits an expert for one future use as readily as for ten. With the whole sequence in view it
runs \fsmRatioGMid$\times$ [\fsmRatioLoGMid, \fsmRatioHiGMid] the online policy at gpt-oss 25\% and
\fsmRatioQMid$\times$ [\fsmRatioLoQMid, \fsmRatioHiQMid] at Qwen3 25\%, closing \fsmClosedGMid{} and
\fsmClosedQMid\% of the gap to this host's limit and recovering \fsmRecoveredGMid{} and \fsmRecoveredQMid\% of the
accounting's foresight term; at the two GPU-bound cells, where the accounting ranks foresight second, it runs
\fsmRatioGHigh$\times$ and \fsmRatioQHigh$\times$. Half of the gpt-oss gain arrives at $W{=}\fsmWfiftyGMid$ tokens,
where the trace study puts $W_{50}$ at 9.4 for this $C/k$ (\cref{sec:traces}); Qwen3's at \fsmWfiftyQMid{} (trace:
3.7). Where the host binds hardest the same oracle \emph{loses}, \fsmLossRange\% at gpt-oss 11\% and Qwen3 12.5\%,
at every window (\fsmNegWinMin--\fsmNegWinMax$\times$). There it reads more host bytes than the online policy,
\fsmReadsOracleGLow{} and \fsmReadsOracleQLow{} experts per token (misses plus admissions) against
\fsmReadsBaseGLow{} and \fsmReadsBaseQLow, because Belady admits an expert used once where the optimum's
\fsmReadsOptGLow{} and \fsmReadsOptQLow{} reads per token come from bypass, and its copies compete with the helpers
for the same memory and land late (\fsmHitOracleGLow{} and \fsmHitOracleQLow\% hits against the optimum's
\fsmHitOptGLow{} and \fsmHitOptQLow\%). The accounting's term is the optimum's reads, foresight in bytes, which a
hit-optimal oracle does not supply; a bytes-optimal one is measured in \cref{app:foresight}. Two interior terms are
tested on the way. Turning off the engine's within-layer overlap of a layer's CPU misses with its GPU hits changes
speed by under 1\% at every cell, so the ``overlap'' the accounting charges is host reads serialised with GPU work
across the step, not within a layer. And the law evaluated on the oracle runs' own counters over-predicts their time
by \fsmLawErrPosMin--\fsmLawErrPosMax\% at the four cells where the oracle gains: the admissions' reads overlapped the
step, which the law charges in sequence. Foresight buys overlap with it.
