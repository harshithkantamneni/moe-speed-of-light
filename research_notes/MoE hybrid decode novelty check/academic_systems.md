# Academic systems and analyses for MoE inference with experts in host memory (2023 to Sept 2026)

Method note: metadata and abstracts come from arXiv abstract pages. Specific numbers, hardware, baselines and configurations come from the full PDF text (pdftotext) of each paper, or from the SOSP'25 PDF for KTransformers. "Not found" means a keyword grep of that paper's text found nothing. It does not prove the paper never mentions the item.

## Q1. Per-paper factual entries (venue/date, mechanism, miss handling, prefetch, hardware, models, baselines, batch-1 decode speedups, llama.cpp comparison)

### Takeaway
About 35 systems and analyses were checked. Until late 2025 most work fetched missed experts to the GPU (cache and prefetch). The CPU-executes-misses line runs Fiddler → KTransformers → HybriMoE/DAOP → the ASP-DAC'26 paper (2512.16473) → DALI → FreeToken. Almost every "llama.cpp" baseline is llama.cpp in static layer-wise mode (`-ngl`), or llama.cpp patched with custom expert offload. Among the system papers checked, none configures the `--n-cpu-moe` / `-ot exps=CPU` expert-only offload at equal VRAM. The one academic paper found using `-ncmoe` is a measurement study, not a system comparison.

### Cited Findings

#### GPU-fetch (cache/prefetch) systems
- **Mixtral-offloading (Eliseev & Mazur)**: technical report, arXiv 28 Dec 2023. Mechanism is a per-layer LRU expert cache, speculative expert loading, and mixed HQQ quantization. Misses are fetched to the GPU. Hardware: T4 (free Colab), RTX 3060, RTX 3080 Mobile, A100-80GB (reference). Model: Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct. Baseline: naive offloading of the entire MoE layer, plus ablations. Full-algorithm tokens/s with 2-bit experts: A100 3.061, 3080 Mobile 2.655, 3060 2.278, T4 (Colab) 2.092. With 3-bit experts: 2.845 / 2.475 / 2.038 / T4 (Cloud) 1.603. "all evaluated setups can generate 2-4 tokens per second". No llama.cpp comparison. — [arXiv 2312.17238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17238)
- **Pre-gated MoE**: ISCA 2024. The ExpertFlow reference list cites it in the "2024 ACM/IEEE 51st Annual" Symposium ([arXiv 2410.17954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17954)). A pre-gate function, which changes the architecture and requires training, selects the next layer's experts so they can be fetched early from CPU memory. Hardware: A100-80GB and AMD EPYC 7V12 over PCIe gen4, FasterTransformer. Models: Switch-Base/Large. Baselines are MoE-OnDemand and MoE-Prefetch. Reported "average 1.7× (max 1.9×) and 42× (max 125×)" improvement versus those two. No llama.cpp comparison. — [arXiv 2308.12066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12066)
- **EdgeMoE**: arXiv Aug 2023, v2 Mar 2025. Non-expert weights stay in device memory; experts are fetched from external storage when activated. Adds expert-wise bitwidth adaptation and expert preloading by prediction. Evaluated on Jetson TX2 and Raspberry Pi 4B, with a reported 1.19×–2.77× inference speedup versus loading-expert and STI baselines. — [arXiv 2308.14352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14352)
- **SwapMoE**: ACL 2024. Keeps a small dynamic set of "Virtual Experts" in memory, so routing is approximate. On Switch Transformer summarization, memory drops from 14.2 GiB to 4.7 GiB with "50% latency reduction" and a Rouge-2 drop of 0.041. — [arXiv 2308.15030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15030)
- **SiDA-MoE**: MLSys 2024. An offline-trained hash-based, data-aware predictor. Reports "up to 3.93× throughput increasing, up to 72% latency reduction". — [arXiv 2310.18859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18859)
- **Towards MoE Deployment (Huang et al.)**: arXiv Mar 2023. Proposes "Expert Buffering", which keeps hot experts in GPU memory and buffers the rest in CPU memory. The paper compares its caching policy with "Belady's MIN" and reports a miss rate "very close to Belady's MIN". — [arXiv 2303.06182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06182)
- **MoE-Infinity**: arXiv Jan 2024, v3 Mar 2025, now titled "Efficient MoE Inference on Personal Machines with Sparsity-Aware Expert Cache". Batch-1 focus. Traces request-level Expert Activation Matrices and uses them to guide cache replacement and prefetching; misses are fetched to the GPU. Hardware: a single RTX A5000 24GB on PCIe 4.0. Models include DeepSeek, Mixtral, Switch, NLLB and Arctic. Reports "3.1–16.7× per-token latency improvements" versus vLLM, Ollama, DeepSpeed and BrainStorm. It describes llama.cpp (Ollama) as "by default … stores all model parameters in CPUs and offloads computations to GPUs". — [arXiv 2401.14361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14361)
- **AdapMoE**: arXiv Aug 2024. Adds sensitivity-based adaptive gating (25% fewer activated experts), adaptive prefetching, and a DP-based per-layer cache allocation. Hardware: RTX 4090 and A6000. Models: Mixtral-8x7B and 8x22B. Reports a "1.35× speedup", which the text states is versus Mixtral-Offloading. No llama.cpp comparison. The ICCAD 2024 venue was not verified. — [arXiv 2408.10284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10284)
- **ProMoE**: arXiv Oct 2024, v3 Sep 2025. A learned MLP predictor (~2M params) running on intermediate results does proactive prefetch into a per-layer LRU cache; misses are fetched. Integrated into both HF transformers and llama.cpp. Hardware: RTX 4090 on PCIe 4.0, batch 1–4. Abstract: "average speedup of 2.20× (up to 3.21×) and 2.07× (up to 5.02×) in the prefill and decode stages". Inside the llama.cpp codebase, ProMoE beats static and LRU caches by 1.49× and 1.09× in decode. The paper also states that llama.cpp's own layer offload (LO, which puts parameters and compute on the CPU) "is significantly faster and even surpasses ProMoE in the decode stage" for Mixtral. — [arXiv 2410.22134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22134)
- **HOBBIT**: arXiv Nov 2024. Mixed precision: cache-miss experts are replaced with low-precision versions. Adds token-level dynamic loading, layer-level prefetch and sequence-level caching, and is built on llama.cpp. Its text also describes a "CPU-GPU cooperative computing mode" in which a CPU helper thread computes a missing expert. Hardware: Jetson AGX Orin 32GB and RTX 4090. Models: Mixtral-8x7B and Phi-MoE. Reports "up to a 9.93x speedup in decoding" versus SOTA offloading. Versus llama.cpp on Jetson Orin it reports averages of 13.0× (Mixtral) and 18.9× (Phi-MoE), which the paper attributes to llama.cpp page faults from limited CPU memory. — [arXiv 2411.01433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01433)
- **ExpertFlow (He et al.)**: DAC'26 per the v2 comment, originally arXiv Oct 2024. A transformer routing-path predictor, token scheduling and a predictive expert cache. Hardware: A40. Reports "reducing GPU memory usage by up to 93.72% and improving inference throughput by up to 10x". This is throughput-oriented. — [arXiv 2410.17954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17954)
- **ExpertFlow (Shen et al.)**: arXiv Oct 2025, a different paper. Adaptive prefetch horizon plus cache-aware routing. Platforms: A100, A6000, RTX 4090. Reports stall time reduced "to less than 0.1% of the baseline". — [arXiv 2510.26730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26730)
- **fMoE / FineMoE**: arXiv Feb 2025, v2 Oct 2025, EuroSys 2026 (the authors' PDF is titled FineMoE_EuroSys26). Fine-grained "expert maps" combined with semantic hints guide prefetch, caching and offload. Prototyped on HF Transformers on a 6× RTX 3090 testbed, plus an A100. Reports "reduces inference latency by 47% and improves expert hit rate by 39%". — [arXiv 2502.05370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05370); [EuroSys'26 PDF](https://intellisys.haow.us/assets/pdf/Hanfei_FineMoE_EuroSys26.pdf)
- **Read-ME**: NeurIPS 2024. A router decoupled from the backbone (pre-gating) enables lookahead scheduling and a "caching strategy inspired by Belady's offline caching algorithm". Reports up to 6.1% mean end-to-end latency improvement. — [arXiv 2410.19123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19123)
- **Mixture of Cache-Conditional Experts (Qualcomm)**: TMLR 06/2025. Cache-aware routing at batch 1 on mobile devices, with "2× speedups". Uses Belady's algorithm as "a theoretical upper bound for cache policy" and claims its approach "surpasses" the oracle cache bound because it changes routing. — [arXiv 2412.00099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00099)
- **SP-MoE**: arXiv Oct 2025. Speculative-decoding-aware expert prefetching, with a "cutoff-layer policy … based on empirical profiles and an analytical latency model". Hardware: RTX 3090, RTX 4090, A100-40GB. Reports "1.07-3.5 times TPOT speedup". — [arXiv 2510.10302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10302)
- **MoE-SpeQ**: arXiv Nov 2025. A draft model predicts the experts of future tokens so they can be prefetched, and an "Amortization Roofline Model" tunes speculation. Reports "at most 2.34x speedup over the state-of-the-art offloading framework" on Phi-MoE. — [arXiv 2511.14102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14102)
- **Speculating Experts**: arXiv Mar 2026. Uses internal representations to speculate future experts and can execute the speculated experts. Hardware: A6000, A100, GH200. Reports "up to 14% reduction in time per output token (TPOT) over on-demand loading of experts from CPU memory". Its analytic model bounds the gain via ΔT = min(t_copy, t_compute), with a maximum speedup of 2×. — [arXiv 2603.19289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19289)
- **SpecMD (Apple)**: arXiv Feb 2026. A benchmarking framework for cache policies plus a "Least-Stale" eviction policy. Evaluated on a single A100 with hardware emulation. Reports "over 88% hit rates with up to 34.7% TTFT reduction on OLMoE at only 5% or 0.6GB of VRAM cache". — [arXiv 2602.03921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03921)
- **SeqMoE**: arXiv 11 Sep 2026. A Mamba2 seq2seq multi-step/multi-layer expert predictor, prefetch scheduling as Job Sequencing with Deadlines, a "probabilistic Belady policy", and a CUDA-graph-compatible runtime. All execution is on the GPU; misses are loaded on demand by a custom kernel from `cudaHostAllocMapped` host memory. Built on HF Transformers hooks. Hardware: RTX 4090 (Qwen3-30B-A3B-FP8), RTX 5090 (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-BF16), RTX PRO 6000 (GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek-V4-Flash). Baselines: llama.cpp, MoE-Infinity, KTransformers, FreeToken, and FullLoad on vLLM. Its Fig. 1 (Qwen3-30B-FP8 on RTX 4090, 45% loaded, single-batch decode) shows Eager 2.3, MoE-Infinity 11.9, "Llama.cpp (Static Offload)" 30.9, KTrans 34.2, SeqMoE 104.1 and FullLoad 122.7 tok/s. Abstract: "With 45% expert residency, SeqMoE averages a 96.97% hit rate and 80.22% of full-load performance". — [arXiv 2609.12978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12978)

#### CPU-executes-misses / CPU-GPU hybrid systems
- **Fiddler**: ICLR 2025, arXiv Feb 2024. Popular experts are placed on the GPU statically, chosen "based on offline profiling of expert popularity" on ShareGPT. For each non-resident expert it chooses between copying weights to the GPU and copying activations to the CPU and computing there; the choice depends on input size, so at batch-1 decode the CPU path wins. Hardware: Quadro RTX 6000 24GB (PCIe Gen3, 56/256 experts on GPU) and RTX 6000 Ada 48GB (Gen4, 125/256 experts). Model: Mixtral-8x7B at 16-bit. Baselines: DeepSpeed-MII (ZeRO-Infinity), Mixtral-Offloading (extended to 16-bit), and llama.cpp b2956 with `ngl` = 8 and 16 layers. Result: "1.26 times faster on average" than the best baseline (llama.cpp) for single-request end-to-end runs. The appendix reports 1.81× (ShareGPT) and 1.56× (LMSYS) versus llama.cpp, and beam search gains 11.57×. — [arXiv 2402.07033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07033)
- **KTransformers**: SOSP 2025. AMX/AVX-512 CPU expert kernels, asynchronous CPU-GPU scheduling with CUDA Graph, and "Expert Deferral". Placement is static and Fiddler-style: routed experts run on the CPU. Hardware: a dual-socket Xeon Platinum machine with an A100-40GB or RTX 4080-16GB. Models: DeepSeek-V3, V2.5, Qwen2-57B. Batch size 1. Decode "speedups ranging from 2.42× to 4.09× over Fiddler and 1.25× to 1.76× over Llama.cpp" at full precision, and "1.77× to 1.93×" over llama.cpp on quantized models. Expert Deferral adds up to 1.45×. Because "Llama.cpp originally only supports layer-wise offloading and lacks expert-level offloading capability", the authors "extended Llama.cpp with custom code to enable expert-level offloading analogous to Fiddler". — [SOSP'25 PDF](https://madsys.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/publication/ktransformers-unleashing-the-full-potential-of-cpu/gpu-hybrid-inference-for-moe-models/SOSP25-chen.pdf); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3731569.3764843)
- **HybriMoE**: DAC 2025, arXiv Apr 2025. Built on kTransformers plus llama.cpp kernels. Combines dynamic intra-layer CPU/GPU scheduling (minimizing max(CPU time, GPU+transfer time) per layer), impact-driven inter-layer prefetch, and a score-based cache. Hardware: RTX A6000 with a Xeon Gold 5220R limited to 10 cores. Models: Mixtral-8x7B, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Qwen2-57B-A14B at cache ratios of 25/50/75%. Baselines: llama.cpp ("statically maps model layers to CPU or GPU"), AdapMoE and kTransformers. Decode is "1.70×" versus kTransformers. The paper notes that llama.cpp "demonstrates relatively strong performance in this [decode] stage". — [arXiv 2504.05897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05897)
- **DAOP**: DATE 2025, arXiv Dec 2024, v3 21 Aug 2026. Assigns experts to CPU or GPU per sequence from activation patterns, with the initial cache calibrated on ShareGPT. Non-cached experts execute on the CPU, and predicted next experts are "pre-calculated" on the CPU with a graceful-degradation (approximate) mechanism. HF Transformers, batch 1. Hardware: RTX A6000 with an i9-10980XE on PCIe 4.0. Models: Mixtral 8x7B and Phi-3.5-MoE. Baselines: MoE-OnDemand, DeepSpeed-MII, Mixtral-Offloading, Fiddler. Reports "outperforms Fiddler by 40.4%" (4.52 tok/s for Mixtral and 8.21 tok/s for Phi-3.5 at [256,512]), a 35.4% average over Fiddler across cache ratios, and in the abstract "up to 8.20× [over caching/prefetching] and offloading techniques by 1.35×". No llama.cpp comparison. — [arXiv 2501.10375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10375)
- **arXiv 2512.16473, "Efficient CPU-GPU Collaborative Inference for MoE-based LLMs on Memory-Limited Systems"**: ASP-DAC 2026, arXiv 18 Dec 2025. A GPU expert cache organised as an "N-index, M-way set-associative structure" (per-layer capacity M) with LRU eviction; LRU and FIFO performed similarly. Hits run on the GPU. Misses are computed on the CPU while the missed expert is asynchronously copied to the GPU for later tokens, so every miss is admitted. It uses two CUDA streams. Hardware: Threadripper 7960X (24-core) with an RTX 4090 on PCIe 4.0 ×16. Models: Mixtral 8x7B and Phi-3.5-MoE. Baselines: on-demand fetching, prefetching, Pre-gated MoE (whose performance is *estimated* "by assuming perfect computation-communication overlap"), and Fiddler. Reports "up to 4.8 and 10.4 tokens per second", "4.4× speedup for Mixtral 8x7B and 4.3× for Phi3.5-MoE" versus the prefetching approach, and "surpassing Fiddler by approximately 1.6×". No llama.cpp baseline. — [arXiv 2512.16473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16473)
- **DALI**: arXiv 3 Feb 2026. KTransformers-based. Assigns experts to CPU or GPU per layer at runtime via a 0-1 integer program solved greedily, and adds Residual-Based Prefetching and Workload-Aware Cache Replacement that exploits "temporal correlation". Hardware: RTX 3090 with an EPYC 7532 (16 cores / 32 threads used). Baselines: llama.cpp, KTransformers, MoE-Lightning, HybriMoE; Fiddler is omitted as "on average 14.3×" slower. Average decode speedups are "3.97×, 2.16×, 1.48×, and 1.32×" over those four baselines, with decode measured "under various batch sizes". llama.cpp and KTransformers are configured by controlling "the number of MoE layers stored and executed on the GPU … to maintain a comparable memory usage". — [arXiv 2602.03495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03495)
- **FreeToken**: arXiv 17 Aug 2026 (Stoica, Zaharia, Han, Keutzer et al.). A shared, all-layer, CUDA-graph-compatible LRU GPU expert cache. Each step's misses are split between cache fill over PCIe and in-place CPU execution, run concurrently, with fill count q⋆ ≈ m·B_P/B_H derived from measured PCIe (B_P) and host expert-kernel (B_H) bandwidths. It "always retains at least one fill". Tested on six machines, from an RTX 4060 laptop to an RTX PRO 6000, with main results on an RTX 5090. Models: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (BF16), DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GLM-5.2. Baselines: llama.cpp, Ollama, KTransformers, MoE-Infinity. Reports "77–83 tok/s on Qwen3.6 and 22–25 tok/s on DSV4-Flash, 1.8–2.3× and 1.5–1.9× the strongest baseline in each workload" (agentic workloads W1–W4). In an equal-capacity trace replay, its global LRU misses 16% and 39% of decode expert reads, versus 41% and 59% for KTransformers' prefill-updated placement. — [arXiv 2608.16157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16157)
- **PowerInfer**: SOSP 2024. Hot neurons are preloaded on the GPU and cold neurons are computed on the CPU. It targets dense ReLU-sparse LLMs, not MoE. RTX 4090, "up to 11.69x" versus llama.cpp. — [arXiv 2312.12456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12456)

#### Throughput-oriented (batched) offloading, not batch-1
- **MoE-Lightning**: arXiv Nov 2024. The ASPLOS 2025 venue was not verified from the arXiv page. CGOPipe CPU-GPU-I/O pipelining plus the HRM "Hierarchical Roofline Model" performance model. Hardware: T4, L4, 2–4×T4. Reports "up to 10.3x higher throughput than state-of-the-art offloading-enabled LLM inference systems for Mixtral 8x7B on a single T4". FlexGen is the main comparison. — [arXiv 2411.11217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11217)
- **MoE-Lens**: arXiv Apr 2025. A holistic performance model giving a "theoretical performance upper bound", evaluated on A40. Reports it "outperforms the state-of-the-art solution by 4.6x on average (up to 25.5x)", with the model "predicting performance with an average 94% accuracy". Baselines are MoE-Lightning and vLLM. — [arXiv 2504.09345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09345)
- **Klotski**: arXiv Feb 2025. Expert-aware multi-batch pipeline, "throughput improvements of up to 85.12x". — [arXiv 2502.06888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06888)
- **MoE-Gen**: arXiv Mar 2025. Module-based batching at "8-31x higher throughput" than FlexGen, MoE-Lightning and DeepSpeed. At batch size one it "defaults to on-demand fetching" yet still beats baselines, which the paper takes to indicate "existing baselines deliver lower throughput compared to a strategy relying purely on on-demand copying". — [arXiv 2503.09716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09716)
- **CoX-MoE**: DAC'26, arXiv May 2026. AMX CPU-GPU co-execution with a static expert stratification. "up to 7.1x and 2.4x higher throughput than FlexGen and MoE-Lightning". — [arXiv 2605.17889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17889)
- **TriMoE**: DAC 2026, arXiv Mar 2026. A GPU / AMX-CPU / DIMM-NDP hot-warm-cold mapping that uses an EMA of expert load. "up to 2.83x speedup". — [arXiv 2603.01058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01058)

#### 2026 measurement and analysis papers
- **Reproducible Evaluation of MoE Expert Caching (Zhang)**: arXiv 8 Aug 2026. A trace-driven, "event-atomic" simulator over Granite-3.1-3B-A800M, OLMoE-1B-7B and Qwen3-30B-A3B (MLX 4-bit). Policies: Belady "offline optimum with bypass admission" and Belady-forced-admit. Reports a remaining gap to the offline optimum of "44.2-45.9%", of which "84.3-96.6%" is attributed to future-victim knowledge. It argues the per-step expert union relative to per-layer capacity must be reported. "every result here is a block count"; there is no real-hardware validation. — [arXiv 2608.07911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07911)
- **Who Should Own the Expert Cache? Kernel-Managed Tiering**: arXiv 12 Aug 2026. Router traces for Qwen3-30B-A3B and DeepSeek-V3 come from "an evaluation callback added to llama.cpp"; a trillion-parameter production model is replayed natively on GH200. Compares static pinning, LFU, LRU and Belady. Derives a promotion "breakeven reuse count" r⋆ = (1/346)/(1/327 − 1/2938) = 1.06. Mentions that "llama.cpp's --cpu-moe places experts in anonymous memory and bypasses the page cache". — [arXiv 2608.12103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12103)
- **Cacheable by Design? (Suram)**: arXiv 18 Aug 2026, pre-registered. Single RTX 3070 8GB. Serves Qwen3-235B-A22B Q4_K_M with "llama.cpp (-ngl 99 -ncmoe 94)". Measured decode of "0.44 tok/s warm, matching a bytes-per-token / bandwidth model". Built "llama-moe-trace", a "∼120-line addition to llama.cpp's eval-callback", and used it on Qwen3-30B-A3B. Per-layer cache simulation at 13.4% budget: LRU 65.9%, LFU 60.1%, static pin 59.2%, Belady oracle 79.1%. — [arXiv 2608.18261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18261)
- **In-depth Analysis on Caching and Pre-fetching in MoE Offloading**: arXiv Nov 2025. LRU vs LFU trace analysis on A100, A6000, L40 and RTX 3090. — [arXiv 2511.05814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05814)
- **Cache-Aware Joint Router Adaptation**: arXiv Sep 2026. Post-training routers for cache hit rate, evaluated in a "trace-driven simulator". The authors state the results "establish neither wall-clock speedup nor energy savings". — [arXiv 2609.04895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04895)
- **Layer-wise MoE Routing Locality under Shared-Prefix Code Generation**: arXiv Apr 2026. Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-FP8 on a GH200 node. Same-token routing Jaccard 0.649 (40× random). — [arXiv 2604.17182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17182)

#### Non-academic but directly relevant prior art
- **llama.cpp RFC Discussion #24528**, "MoE expert cache, VRAM caching of hot CPU-resident experts with hybrid hit/miss execution": posted by @leloch on June 12, 2026. A persistent VRAM buffer caches experts. MUL_MAT_ID stays on the CPU, with "thread 0 dispatch[ing] cached (hit) rows to GPU while other threads compute misses". Reported gains: "+25%" (GLM-5.1 754B IQ2_M), "+7%" (Qwen3.5 397B), and "+10%…+57%" over 13 forced-offload models. Independent reports: "+28% to +46%" (2× RTX 3090) and "+21% to +52%" (RTX 5090). — [GitHub discussion #24528](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/24528)

### Inferences
- In batch-1 decode on consumer PCIe links, published designs split into two camps. The fetch camp (MoE-Infinity, ProMoE, HOBBIT's main mode, SeqMoE) relies on high hit rates plus prediction. The CPU-miss camp (Fiddler, KTransformers, HybriMoE, DAOP, 2512.16473, DALI, FreeToken) relies on the CPU's memory bandwidth. SeqMoE's 104 tok/s comes from a GPU-only design on RTX 4090-class GPUs with PCIe 4.0 and FP8 weights at 45% residency, so it is not directly comparable to an A10 at equal VRAM.
- None of the papers examined reports results on an NVIDIA A10 (the hardware grep found none).

### Gaps
- Venues for MoE-Lightning (ASPLOS 2025?), AdapMoE (ICCAD 2024?) and EdgeMoE's later journal version were not verified from primary pages.
- HybriMoE's and DALI's absolute decode speedups versus llama.cpp at batch 1 are only in figures, so no exact numbers could be quoted.
- SeqMoE and FreeToken do not state, in the passages examined, which llama.cpp flags (`-ngl`, `-ot`, `--n-cpu-moe`) they used.
- Not examined in full text: MoE-Beyond, Fate, Lynx, eMoE, FloE, MoE-APEX, Patterns-MoE, PreScope, Oracle-MoE, StickyMoE, and "Two-Stage Expert Offloading for Domain-Aware MoE Inference". They are cited by other papers as related prediction or caching work.
- A GitHub issue (ddvnguyen/llama.cpp #130, "MoE expert-cache decode throughput on a PCIe x4 RTX 3060") surfaced in search but was not read.
- No MLSys 2026, OSDI 2026 or ATC 2026 paper specific to batch-1 hybrid MoE decode was identified. FineMoE is EuroSys 2026; ExpertFlow, TriMoE and CoX-MoE are DAC 2026; 2512.16473 is ASP-DAC 2026.

## Q2. Which papers combine a dynamic GPU expert cache with CPU-executed misses? Which do CPU/GPU concurrency within a layer?

### Takeaway
The combination of a dynamic GPU expert cache, hits on the GPU and misses on the CPU is already published several times. The most direct instances are 2512.16473 (ASP-DAC 2026: per-layer set-associative LRU cache with admit-on-miss async fill) and FreeToken (Aug 2026: LRU cache with a bandwidth-derived fill-vs-CPU split). HybriMoE, DAOP and DALI are close variants, and the llama.cpp RFC #24528 (June 2026) does the same thing inside llama.cpp. Intra-layer CPU/GPU concurrency is also standard in HybriMoE, DALI, FreeToken and RFC #24528.

### Cited Findings
- 2512.16473: "When required experts are not cached on GPU, computations are offloaded to CPU while experts are asynchronously fetched to GPU for subsequent token generation". The cache is an "N-index, M-way set-associative structure" with "an LRU eviction policy". — [arXiv 2512.16473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16473)
- FreeToken splits misses into "a cache-fill set F and a CPU-execution set C"; "These two sets are served concurrently". The split follows q⋆ ≈ m·B_P/B_H, and the cache uses LRU with "at least one fill" per step. — [arXiv 2608.16157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16157)
- HybriMoE uses a "dynamic intra-layer scheduling strategy to balance workloads across CPU and GPU" together with "score-based caching". Its scheduling objective minimizes the max of CPU time and GPU+PCIe time within a layer. — [arXiv 2504.05897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05897)
- DALI "dynamically assigns experts to CPU or GPU by modeling assignment as a 0-1 integer optimization problem" and adds "Workload-Aware Cache Replacement". Its objective is min max(T_gpu, T_cpu), and cached experts can "cooperate with our Greedy Assignment strategy". — [arXiv 2602.03495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03495)
- DAOP "dynamically allocates experts between CPU and GPU based on per-sequence activation patterns, and selectively pre-calculates predicted experts on CPUs". — [arXiv 2501.10375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10375)
- HOBBIT describes a "CPU-GPU cooperative computing mode": "if the required expert is not in GPU memory, the main thread sends the expert's input to the CPU, where a helper thread processes it". — [arXiv 2411.01433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01433)
- Fiddler keeps a *static* popularity-profiled GPU set and runs non-resident experts on the CPU at small input sizes. KTransformers and CoX-MoE also use static or pre-assigned GPU sets. — [Fiddler](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07033); [KTransformers SOSP'25](https://madsys.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/publication/ktransformers-unleashing-the-full-potential-of-cpu/gpu-hybrid-inference-for-moe-models/SOSP25-chen.pdf); [CoX-MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17889)
- In llama.cpp RFC #24528, "thread 0 dispatches cached (hit) rows to GPU while other threads compute misses" (June 2026). — [GitHub #24528](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/24528)
- For GPU-to-CPU signalling, SeqMoE uses `cudaHostAllocMapped` pinned mapped memory as "a shared control region between CPU and GPU": the GPU updates the inference position each layer and sets flags that the CPU prefetcher reads. This serves prefetch, not CPU compute. KTransformers uses "an asynchronous CPU-GPU task scheduling mechanism" with CUDA Graph. — [SeqMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12978); [KTransformers](https://madsys.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/publication/ktransformers-unleashing-the-full-potential-of-cpu/gpu-hybrid-inference-for-moe-models/SOSP25-chen.pdf)
- Frequency- or age-weighted scoring appears in several places. 2608.07911 evaluates "LFRU (rank by frequency / age)", TriMoE uses an "Exponential Moving Average (EMA) for each expert's load", and HybriMoE uses score-based caching. — [2608.07911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07911); [TriMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01058); [HybriMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05897)

### Inferences
- The system in the compared work (per-layer cache, decayed-frequency admission, CPU-executed misses, GPU-signalled hand-off) is architecturally a close cousin of 2512.16473 (per-layer, but admit-always LRU) and FreeToken (global LRU plus bandwidth split). Its differentiators would have to be (a) the admission rule, i.e. decayed frequency with bypass rather than admit-on-miss, (b) the hand-off mechanism and its overhead, and (c) the equal-VRAM comparison against `--n-cpu-moe`. The high-level combination itself cannot be claimed as new.
- The admission/bypass idea connects to 2608.07911's finding that bypass admission accounts for only 15.7% (B=8) to 3.4% (B=2) of the Belady gap in its simulated settings. In those settings, an admission-only policy can capture only a modest share of the offline headroom.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether any paper uses a *decayed-frequency admission filter* specifically; I found no exact phrase match. HybriMoE's score and DALI's workload-aware replacement are the closest and were not read in full detail.
- Could not verify KTransformers' exact CPU hand-off signalling mechanism beyond "asynchronous CPU-GPU task scheduling" and CUDA Graph.

## Q3. Analytic performance models, upper bounds (roofline, Belady), and trace studies of expert locality; comparison with a Belady+Jensen speed-of-light bound

### Takeaway
Roofline-style throughput models (MoE-Lightning's HRM, MoE-Lens, CoX-MoE, TriMoE, MoE-SpeQ) and Belady-based hit-rate/traffic bounds (from 2303.06182 in 2023 to 2608.07911, 2608.12103, 2608.18261 and SeqMoE in 2026) are both well established. Belady with bypass admission is published: 2608.07911 uses it explicitly, per layer, with a forced-admit decomposition. A simple bytes-per-token/bandwidth decode model is published and was matched on one configuration (2608.18261). I did not find a paper that converts the Belady miss bound into a *decode-time* speed-of-light bound for any per-layer-capacity policy with CPU-executed misses (load accounting plus Jensen). Nor did I find one that validates a decode model against dozens of published measurements from other systems.

### Cited Findings
- MoE-Lightning introduces "a performance model, HRM, based on a Hierarchical Roofline Model" for policy search (throughput, batched). — [arXiv 2411.11217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11217)
- MoE-Lens builds a "holistic performance model … to understand the theoretical performance upper bound of MoE inference", with "an average 94% accuracy". It argues that prior "performance models with limited scope … neither identify nor achieve the hardware limit" (throughput). — [arXiv 2504.09345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09345)
- CoX-MoE uses a GPU/AMX roofline and a lower bound T_tot ≥ N × min(T_l). TriMoE uses an "Empirical GPU-CPU-NDP roofline". MoE-SpeQ uses an "Amortization Roofline Model". SP-MoE uses "an analytical latency model". Speculating Experts gives a TPOT model whose improvement is bounded by min(t_copy, t_compute), with a maximum speedup of 2×. — [CoX-MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17889); [TriMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01058); [MoE-SpeQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14102); [SP-MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10302); [Speculating Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19289)
- FreeToken models branch times as T_fill(q) ≈ qS/B_P and T_cpu(m−q) ≈ (m−q)S/(B_H − B_P), which are bytes-over-bandwidth terms, and balances them. — [arXiv 2608.16157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16157)
- 2608.18261: "measured decode is 0.44 tok/s warm, in exact agreement with a bytes-per-token ÷ bandwidth model". The "first-principles model tok/s = bandwidth/bytes-per-token predicts 0.20 tok/s from cold disk". — [arXiv 2608.18261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18261)
- Belady usage over time:
  - Towards MoE Deployment (2023) compares Expert Buffering against "the theoretical optimal Belady's MIN". — [arXiv 2303.06182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06182)
  - Read-ME (NeurIPS 2024) uses a "caching strategy inspired by Belady's offline caching algorithm". — [arXiv 2410.19123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19123)
  - Cache-conditional experts (TMLR 2025) treats Belady as "a theoretical upper bound for cache policy". — [arXiv 2412.00099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00099)
  - 2608.07911 evaluates "Belady (offline optimum with bypass admission), and Belady-forced-admit", with per-layer scope primary, and reports a "recoverable gap" g = (m_best-causal − m_Belady)/m_best-causal. — [arXiv 2608.07911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07911)
  - 2608.12103 compares policies against "Belady's offline optimum", with LRU reaching "approximately 70% of Belady's" at one scale. — [arXiv 2608.12103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12103)
  - 2608.18261 reports "Belady oracle @ 13.4% budget 79.1%" (per-layer cache simulation). — [arXiv 2608.18261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18261)
  - SeqMoE compares against "variants of the optimal Belady's MIN" (Belady, Belady-8, Belady-F8) and proposes a "probabilistic Belady policy". — [arXiv 2609.12978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12978)
- 2608.07911 says its "every result here is a block count, and the step to service quality passes through overlap, topology and contention we do not model". It lists "real-hardware validation" as the limitation it would most want removed. — [arXiv 2608.07911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07911)
- 2608.07911 audited ten papers and found "none of the ten directly reports the measured per-step/per-layer expert union divided by usable per-layer capacity". — [arXiv 2608.07911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07911)
- Trace/locality studies:
  - 2608.18261 on Qwen3-30B-A3B: "adjacent-token expert reuse is 2.0x chance, 95% of traffic uses 52.5% of experts, and an LRU cache of 13.4% of experts serves 66% of requests". — [arXiv 2608.18261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18261)
  - 2608.12103: coverage "continues to grow without saturating", and "each additional step reuses recently selected experts at approximately 99%". — [arXiv 2608.12103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12103)
  - 2512.16473: "approximately 44% of the time, routers direct experts to the same ID as in the previous block", and experts are "re-employed in subsequent token generation with 30% to 50% probability" (Mixtral). — [arXiv 2512.16473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16473)
  - SpecMD: MoE expert access "is not consistent with temporal locality assumptions (e.g LRU, LFU)". — [arXiv 2602.03921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03921)

### Inferences
- A "Belady MIN with bypass" bound on transferred blocks per layer is not novel on its own; 2608.07911 is the closest prior art and is very close in framing. The potentially novel pieces are:
  - translating the per-layer miss/traffic bound into a decode-*time* speed-of-light for any per-layer-capacity placement, including CPU-executed misses and per-step load accounting;
  - the Jensen step, which lets a bound from averages hold over variable per-step loads.
  
  These fill the "block count → service time" gap that 2608.07911 explicitly leaves open.
- Validation of a decode model on 52 *published* measurements with held-out error has no counterpart I found. The closest are MoE-Lens (94% accuracy on its own system, throughput regime) and 2608.18261 (one configuration).
- The SpecMD claim that access patterns defy LRU/LFU assumptions conflicts in emphasis with 2608.18261 and 2608.12103, which both find strong short-range reuse. The difference is plausibly model- and regime-dependent, which is 2608.07911's central argument.

### Gaps
- No paper found that applies Jensen's inequality to MoE offloading bounds; the grep for "jensen" across all texts returned nothing.
- MoE-Lens and MoE-Lightning model internals were not read in depth, so it is unconfirmed whether either contains an explicit batch-1 decode bound.

## Q4. Which papers collect routing traces, and how (GPU runs vs CPU)? Is layer-streamed teacher-forced tracing known?

### Takeaway
Trace collection in the literature is done by running the model end to end in a serving engine: HF/PyTorch hooks, llama.cpp eval-callback, vLLM-like engines, MLX, or a production engine on GH200. It is typically GPU-hosted, or llama.cpp with most weights on CPU/SSD. SeqMoE (Sept 2026) already describes teacher-forced *prefill replay* of previously generated token IDs to dump expert activations, but runs it through the GPU inference engine. None of the papers examined describes collecting exact routing traces without a GPU by streaming one decoder layer at a time.

### Cited Findings
- SeqMoE: "we adopt a two-pass process. The first pass collects token ID traces through continuous batching. The second packs multiple token ID traces into a single prefill pass to dump expert activations, fully exploiting the inference engine's parallelism." It uses a 50K-trace pool from MATH, GSM8K, CodeForces, OpenOrca and ShareGPT. — [arXiv 2609.12978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12978)
- 2608.18261: "llama-moe-trace, a ∼120-line addition to llama.cpp's eval-callback that records the router's top-k selection (ffn moe topk) for every layer and token". It warns that the tensor "is a non-contiguous view … a flat copy read whole argsort rows and produced perfectly uniform garbage". — [arXiv 2608.18261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18261)
- 2608.12103: "Qwen3-30B-A3B and 671B DeepSeek-V3 are traced through an evaluation callback added to llama.cpp"; "The production-model traces come from its serving engine". — [arXiv 2608.12103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12103)
- 2608.07911: "Decode routing was collected autoregressively with the model's chat template applied … greedy decoding … up to 384 recorded decode forwards per request". It stores "complete per-layer router logits, top-k indices, probabilities, gate weights", with Qwen3 from an "MLX Qwen3 snapshot (4-bit)". — [arXiv 2608.07911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07911)
- MoE-Infinity traces sparse expert activation online during inference with an "activation tracer" that builds Expert Activation Matrices. — [arXiv 2401.14361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14361)
- ProMoE splits "collected traces" of gate outputs into training and evaluation sets for its predictor. — [arXiv 2410.22134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22134)
- Fiddler's popularity profile "is generated by running inferences" on ShareGPT samples. — [arXiv 2402.07033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07033)
- FreeToken "replays identical routing traces from all four workloads against the three engines' placement policies at equal cache capacity". — [arXiv 2608.16157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16157)
- Layer-by-layer weight streaming exists for *execution*, not tracing. MoE-Lightning notes weights "are loaded layer-by-layer to the GPU for computation" in FlexGen-style batch offloading, and Mixtral-offloading describes prefill as encoded "in parallel (layer-by-layer)". — [MoE-Lightning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11217); [Mixtral-offloading](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17238)
- "Teacher-forced" appears elsewhere only for *predictor training*: SeqMoE trains its predictor "with teacher forcing", and 2609.04895 uses "teacher-forced training supervision". — [SeqMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12978); [2609.04895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04895)

### Inferences
- "Replay generated tokens as a teacher-forced prefill to obtain exact routing" is now published (SeqMoE, Sept 2026), so that element is not novel by itself.
- What appears unpublished in this literature is the GPU-free, one-decoder-layer-at-a-time streamed version. Its value is exact traces for models far larger than available accelerator or host memory, with no GPU. The novelty claim should be narrowed to that mechanism and its exactness guarantees.

### Gaps
- It is unknown what hardware SeqMoE used for trace dumping, though it is implied to be the same GPU platforms.
- Tools like AirLLM (layered inference) were not checked as prior art for layer-streamed execution, because they are not academic papers in scope.

## Q5. Typical speedup magnitudes, against which baselines; critiques of weak baselines; comparisons with llama.cpp `--n-cpu-moe` / static layer offload at equal memory

### Takeaway
Large multipliers (4×–85×) are almost always against naive/on-demand offloading, DeepSpeed-style ZeRO offload, Mixtral-Offloading, or throughput systems like FlexGen. Against strong CPU-compute hybrids the gains shrink to about 1.1×–2.3×: Fiddler's 1.26× over llama.cpp, KTransformers' 1.25–1.76×, HybriMoE's 1.70× over KTransformers, DALI's 1.32× over HybriMoE, FreeToken's 1.5–2.3×, and RFC #24528's +7% to +57%. llama.cpp is almost always configured as whole-layer static offload, or patched for expert-level offload. An explicit equal-VRAM comparison against `--n-cpu-moe` was not found in any system paper examined.

### Cited Findings
- Large multipliers against weak baselines:

  - MoE-Lightning: 10.3× versus FlexGen-class systems. — [arXiv 2411.11217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11217)
- 2512.16473 reports its 4.4× against Pre-gated MoE, whose performance it *estimates* "by assuming perfect computation-communication overlap" because the implementation "does not support these newer models". — [arXiv 2512.16473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16473)

  - ProMoE: llama.cpp's layer offload with CPU compute "is significantly faster and even surpasses ProMoE in the decode stage" on Mixtral. — [arXiv 2410.22134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22134)

### Inferences
- A 1.41–1.81× gain over `--n-cpu-moe` at equal VRAM sits in the same band as the strongest-baseline results: Fiddler 1.26×, KTransformers 1.25–1.76×, FreeToken 1.5–2.3×, and RFC #24528's +7% to +57%. It is plausible, and not an outlier.
- The equal-VRAM-against-`--n-cpu-moe` methodology is a genuine gap in the academic literature, and a defensible contribution. Most papers use whole-layer `-ngl` offload (which also moves attention and KV to the CPU for offloaded layers), a patched llama.cpp, or unspecified configurations.
- The Fiddler (2024) and KTransformers (2025) llama.cpp baselines predate or bypass expert-only offload, so their llama.cpp numbers likely understate modern llama.cpp. This follows from KTransformers' own statement that llama.cpp "originally only supports layer-wise offloading".

### Gaps
- Could not determine from the text examined which llama.cpp build and flags SeqMoE, FreeToken and DALI used, or whether any used `-ot exps=CPU` or `--n-cpu-moe`.
- The RFC #24528 baseline flags were not confirmed beyond "forced offload".

## Q6. Which ideas of the compared work are already published?

### Takeaway
Most individual ingredients have close published precedents, several of them from Aug–Sep 2026:
- per-layer Belady with bypass (2608.07911);
- breakeven reuse count of the same algebraic form as r* (2608.12103);
- teacher-forced prefill replay for traces (SeqMoE);
- dynamic GPU expert cache with CPU-executed misses (2512.16473, FreeToken, HybriMoE, DALI, DAOP, llama.cpp RFC #24528);
- bytes/bandwidth decode model (2608.18261, FreeToken).

What remains distinctive:
- GPU-free, layer-streamed exact tracing;
- a decode-time speed-of-light bound for any per-layer-capacity placement, with CPU-executed misses and load accounting via Jensen;
- validation of a decode model on 52 published measurements;
- decayed-frequency admission specifically;
- a rigorous equal-VRAM comparison against `--n-cpu-moe` on an A10.

### Cited Findings
- (1) Traces: SeqMoE's two-pass prefill dump of generated token IDs. llama.cpp eval-callback tracers (2608.18261, 2608.12103). Autoregressive MLX collection (2608.07911). — [SeqMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12978); [2608.18261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18261); [2608.12103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12103); [2608.07911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07911)
- (2a) Bytes/bandwidth decode model: 2608.18261 ("tok/s = bandwidth/bytes-per-token", matched at 0.44 tok/s) and FreeToken's T = bytes/bandwidth branch model. Throughput-regime models: MoE-Lightning HRM, MoE-Lens (94% accuracy). — [2608.18261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18261); [FreeToken](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16157); [MoE-Lightning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11217); [MoE-Lens](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09345)
- (2b) Belady MIN with bypass, per-layer scope: 2608.07911 ("offline optimum with bypass admission", plus forced-admit decomposition and union-to-capacity ratio). Other Belady uses: 2303.06182, Read-ME, 2412.00099, 2608.12103, 2608.18261, SeqMoE. — [2608.07911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07911); [2303.06182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06182); [Read-ME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19123); [2412.00099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00099); [SeqMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12978)
- (2c) Reuse threshold: 2608.12103 says "Promoting an expert of S bytes to HBM costs one overlapped transfer S/346 and saves S(1/327 − 1/2938) on each subsequent use … The breakeven reuse count is r⋆ = (1/346)/(1/327 − 1/2938) = 1.06". Related decision rules: Fiddler's per-expert CPU-vs-GPU choice by input size, and FreeToken's q⋆ ≈ m·B_P/B_H. — [2608.12103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12103); [Fiddler](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07033); [FreeToken](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16157)
- (3) Cache plus CPU misses: 2512.16473 (per-layer set-associative LRU, CPU misses, async fill), FreeToken (LRU plus concurrent CPU/fill split), HybriMoE (score-based cache plus intra-layer scheduling), DALI, DAOP, HOBBIT's cooperative mode, and llama.cpp RFC #24528 (hits on GPU, misses on CPU threads). — [2512.16473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16473); [FreeToken](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16157); [HybriMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05897); [DALI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03495); [DAOP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10375); [HOBBIT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01433); [llama.cpp #24528](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/24528)

### Inferences
- r* = p/(b−a) matches 2608.12103's r⋆ in structure: one-time transfer cost divided by per-use saving. The compared work should cite 2608.12103 and frame its r* as applying that breakeven to CPU-executed-miss versus GPU-hit service times (PCIe copy versus CPU-DRAM streaming versus HBM), not as a new concept.
- 2608.07911 is the closest prior art to the Belady bound and should be cited and differentiated. It stays in block counts under a fused-event contract with no hardware validation. The compared work's bound is in time, covers CPU-executed misses, applies to any per-layer-capacity policy, and is checked against hardware.
- For the system, the defensible novelty is in the specifics: decayed-frequency admission with bypass, informed by the r* threshold; the low-overhead GPU-signalled CPU hand-off; and the first equal-VRAM head-to-head against llama.cpp's `--n-cpu-moe` on a datacenter A10. The claim should not rest on the "cache + CPU misses" combination, which 2512.16473 (Dec 2025), FreeToken (Aug 2026) and llama.cpp RFC #24528 (June 2026) have already published.

### Gaps
- Dates matter for priority. 2608.07911 (8 Aug 2026), 2608.12103 (12 Aug 2026), FreeToken (17 Aug 2026), 2608.18261 (18 Aug 2026) and SeqMoE (11 Sep 2026) are all very recent. The compared work's own public timestamp was not provided, so relative priority cannot be assessed here.
